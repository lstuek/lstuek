# City banner: build and test decisions

Product spec: [banner.md](banner.md). Same decisions and revision as the product spec.

## Implementation Decisions

- Format: animated WebP, 1200 by 600, 12 seconds at 24 frames per second (288 distinct frames; the last frame is not a copy of the first), looping forever. Frame delays alternate 41 and 42 ms so the loop sums to exactly 12 seconds; billboard slots are 41 or 42 frames. Target 5 MB, hard limit under 10 MB. GIF is the fallback only if WebP fails on GitHub. No film grain (it compresses badly).
- Playback proof first: before any full render, a dummy loop at 4 MB and 8 MB is uploaded as a test release and shown on a test branch's README, checked on desktop, phone browser and the GitHub mobile app, then deleted. The live profile page does not change.
- Scene and look (Blender, on the PC): one still camera on one dense street built from the six CC-BY models plus Blender curves for cables. Look pass: volumetric fog, bloom, colour grade, depth of field, light chromatic aberration, wet-road reflections, teal and magenta with orange accents on a blue-black sky. Decorative East Asian signs use real words or abstract glyphs, never wrong characters. The billboard model's anime artwork and any real-shop textures are replaced. The Blender scene and models stay on the PC, outside the public repo; the build and render scripts live in the repo.
- Composition: the billboard is the hero, at least 40% of the frame width and facing the camera; the two number signs come second; the three holograms third, translucent, names only, on dark smoked plates; filler signs dimmer and out of focus. Text keeps 5% from every edge. The car and drone never cross the billboard while a name is showing. A 12 to 16 px inner vignette keeps the frame edge clean on GitHub's dark and light themes.
- Loop safety: every simulation (rain, steam, flicker, car, drone) is periodic over exactly 12 seconds or resets while hidden; a check compares the seam frame difference with the average neighbouring-frame difference, and two loops are watched back to back. Frame 1 is calm and complete (both numbers, billboard on Portfolio, no flicker, no sweep), because some viewers show only the first frame.
- Flash safety: at most 3 flashes in any 1 second; neon dips go no deeper than 30% and never to black; at most one event (billboard switch, sweep, car pass, neon dip) in any 1 second. The sweep is one directional wipe in and one out over about 0.5 seconds, on a mid-dark background.
- Base render outputs (the contract between Blender and the nightly overlay), all at final resolution: the beauty frames with sign faces dark but lit by a placeholder tube shape of the same size and colour; the wireframe pass of the same frames (a line pass, or an edge pass from depth and normals if the wireframe turns to grey noise when shrunk); per text area a foreground mask per frame (cars, steam, cables in front); a rain-only layer to draw above the text; one shared flicker curve; and a spot file giving, for each of the six text areas (two signs, three holograms, one billboard), a unique id, kind, four on-screen corners (inside the frame, non-zero area), colour, blur amount and its wet-road reflection area.
- City data: all-time tokens (Claude plus Codex) from the existing PC data, and total commits, defined as Lincoln's own non-bot commits on the default branches of the repos the job lists. Total commits comes from the city data change (lstuek/lstuek PR #14), a hard dependency; New Era is removed from its keys. A failed or partial fetch stops the run; it never publishes a lower number.
- Text (on the 1200 canvas, so it stays at least 12 px tall on a phone, at about 0.30 scale): sign numbers at least 60 px cap height, hologram names at least 40 px, billboard names at least 50 px. Signs are a number plus one word ("23.4B TOKENS", "1,532 COMMITS"), with the unit as large as the number and wide tracking. Each area has a fixed fit cell sized for its worst case ("104.2B", "10,000"). Neon type: a tube font with a white-hot core and a coloured halo.
- Overlay plan (pure logic, no images): from the city data, the fixed project names and the spot file, it returns what text shows where on every frame: the two numbers, the three hologram names, the billboard schedule (seven names, about 1.7 seconds each, first slot Portfolio), the shared flicker, and the sweep window. During the sweep, text under the scan line turns to a cyan outline driven by the same mask as the line. It refuses any string that is not a formatted number or an approved name.
- Compositor: warps each text into its area in perspective, adds glow after the warp, cuts it with the foreground mask, adds a matching faint reflection in the area's road patch, draws the rain layer on top, blends the wireframe behind the scan line, and encodes the WebP with exact frame delays. It runs in the PC job's Python with pinned image libraries that can write animated WebP. A preflight checks the interpreter, the libraries, the fonts, the base render files, disk space and release access before work starts. The full 288-frame build must finish within 5 minutes on the PC.
- Project text under the banner (Lincoln's words, fixed): Portfolio "Check out my work and offers." Bionic Wave "AI design agency. I overhauled their flagship site." KA Tech "Performance airsoft rifles. I built their Shopify store." KA Ops "KA Tech's team dashboard for orders, stock and expenses." aios "A custom built AI ecosystem that lets me ship faster than ever." CRM "My personal CRM to analyze the relations of my contacts, and keep in touch." SystemSelect "A software finder that ranks tools by fit, never by pay." Public projects are linked; the rest are plain text.
- Publishing: each run uploads a new banner with a unique name (date plus run id) to one release of the profile repo, checks that the uploaded file is served, and only then updates the README image link and alt text. The last three good banners are kept; older ones are deleted. Any failure leaves the README on the last good banner. The privacy guard allows exactly this README change (banner link and alt text) and the approved names; it still blocks any other name. The six-hourly PC task builds the banner once per night.
- Profile swap: after Lincoln approves the finished loop, the README shows the banner linked to lincolnstuek.com, the project list, and a text credits link. The card workflow's triggers are switched off and any running publish finishes first; the card publisher is archived with a recovery note.
- Credits: a credits file lists each model's title, author, source link, license and license link, and what was changed, with the license evidence saved at download time.
- Sync recovery comes first: the PC clone's unsynced ledger history is reconciled (PR #16 restores it) and kept before the clone is reset, then one scheduled run must upload successfully.
- Accepted limit: the nightly numbers do not reflect in puddles or light the street; each sign keeps a fixed ambient glow. Lincoln approves this on a wet-street still. The full nightly re-render stays an upgrade path, not part of this build.

## Testing Decisions

- A good test checks what a visitor or Lincoln would notice, never how the code is built inside. Each check is shown failing on a deliberately broken input and passing on a good one.
- One end-to-end check builds the banner from a tiny fixture base render and fixture data and checks the file: animated WebP, loops forever, expected size and frame count, delays summing to the loop length, the expected text drawn in each area (compared with a known patch for known numbers), the wireframe appearing only in its window (with a fixture wireframe that a plain cyan patch cannot imitate), and text hidden where the fixture mask covers it.
- Small checks: number formatting (23.4B, 1,532, worst cases fit their cell, a missing value never shows 0), the privacy refusal, and the spot file (six unique ids, valid kinds, corners inside the frame, non-zero area).
- Publishing failure checks: a failed upload, a failed serve check, a failed README push and a retry each leave the last good banner in place.
- Measured once on the real scene: the full-size file size, the build time, the seam check, and phone legibility (stills reduced to 358 px wide and read).
- The look is judged by Lincoln on rendered stills, then on the finished loop. Prior art: the repo's pytest suite (the PC job and card tests).
